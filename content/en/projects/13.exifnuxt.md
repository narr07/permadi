## Photo Metadata Matters More Than It Looks

Every photo carries hidden notes: camera make, lens, exposure settings, time, sometimes GPS coordinates. Those notes help with archiving and copyright, yet they can also reveal where you live once a photo is shared. Changing them usually means command-line tools that aren't friendly to beginners.

**ExifNuxt** lays all of that metadata out in a clean form. Fix wrong values, add the photographer and copyright, or strip every EXIF tag before a photo goes online.

## Key Features

- **Open photos quickly**: use the dialog (`Ctrl+O`) or drag files onto the window. Supports JPEG, PNG, WebP, TIFF, HEIC/HEIF, and JPEG XL.
- **Per-file editing**: camera, lens, exposure settings, time, photographer, copyright, description, and GPS location.
- **Batch editing**: tick several photos and change only the fields you want to match. Every other field on each photo stays as it was.
- **Copy and paste fields**: copy one group (Location, for example) or every field onto other photos. The text also lands on the system clipboard.
- **Safe saving**: `Ctrl+S` overwrites the original, `Ctrl+Shift+S` saves a copy, and you can strip all EXIF in one step.

::tip
ExifNuxt writes only the fields you actually changed, so untouched camera data stays intact.
::

## Technology

The interface uses **Nuxt 4** and **Nuxt UI 4** on top of **Tauri 2**. On the Rust side, `kamadak-exif` reads EXIF and `little_exif` writes it. For PNG files, EXIF goes into a zTXt chunk, following the ImageMagick and exiftool convention.
