**Summative assessment is** the evaluation of student learning outcomes conducted at the end of an instructional period, such as the end of a topic, semester, or educational stage. This evaluation compares student achievements against Learning Objective (TP) benchmarks to determine report card grades, grade promotion, or graduation from an educational institution.

Many educators still equate this final evaluation solely with multiple-choice tests held simultaneously at the end of the semester. However, the spirit of the Merdeka Curriculum shifts the focus of assessment from merely collecting numbers to demonstrating authentic competence. This article explores the concept, latest regulations, how to create instruments, and concrete examples of grading in the classroom.

## Understanding Summative Assessment in the Merdeka Curriculum

Referring to the *2025 Revised Learning and Assessment Guide* published by BSKAP Kemendikbudristek, summative assessment functions to measure student learning achievements after completing one or more learning objectives. Unlike formative assessment, which monitors the process, summative evaluation focuses on the final product of student understanding (assessment of learning).

![Visual comparison diagram contrasting formative progress monitoring and final summative evaluation](/article/edu-asesmen-sumatif-merdeka-ilustrasi.webp)

Grant Wiggins and Jay McTighe in their classic book, *Understanding by Design*, make a clear distinction between continuous assessment and final evaluation. They explain that summative evaluation is credential-related, confirming whether a learner has truly mastered the set competency standards before moving to the next stage.

The function of this assessment also adapts to the education level. In primary and secondary education (SD, SMP, SMA/SMK), summative results serve as the main basis for determining grade promotion and graduation. Meanwhile, in early childhood education (PAUD), summative assessment is used to determine the achievement of a child's developmental phase, not as a selection tool for grade promotion.

### Fundamental Differences Between Formative and Summative Assessments

To prevent overlap when processing grades at the end of the semester, teachers need to separate formative and summative portions right from the start when designing a [Merdeka Curriculum teaching module](/blog/merdeka-teaching-module-guide). Scores from daily quizzes or formative practice discussions must never be averaged into report card grades.

If you want to enrich the variety of daily learning monitoring, please read our review on [practical formative assessment examples for teachers in the classroom](/blog/formative-assessment-in-classroom). The table below summarizes the boundary between these two types of assessment:

| Comparison Aspect | Formative Assessment (Assessment for/as Learning) | Summative Assessment (Assessment of Learning) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | At the beginning and throughout the learning process | At the end of a topic scope (TP), end of the semester, or end of a phase |
| **Primary Goal** | To monitor learning progress, student reflection, and improve teacher instruction | To measure the achievement of Learning Objectives compared to standards (KKTP) |
| **Grade Function** | Diagnostic feedback and qualitative narrative support | The only source for calculating numerical grades and descriptions on the report card |
| **Follow-up** | Immediate adjustment of teaching strategies in the classroom | Remedial for unmastered indicators and learning achievement reports |

## Latest Regulations and the Deep Learning Paradigm in Assessment

Since Permendikbudristek Number 12 of 2024 established the Merdeka Curriculum as the national curriculum, educators hold full autonomy in designing assessments. This rule is strengthened by the *2025 Revised Learning and Assessment Guide*, which promotes the Deep Learning paradigm. Assessment no longer tests shallow memorization but demands mastery of essential concepts and their real-world applications.

There are two highly critical regulatory shifts that every classroom and subject teacher must understand:

1. **Replacing KKM with KKTP:** The Minimum Mastery Criteria (KKM), a rigid single number uniform across the school, is officially removed. Instead, teachers establish Learning Objective Achievement Criteria (KKTP) in the form of criteria descriptions, rubrics, or grade intervals.
2. **Optional End of Semester Summative (SAS):** Schools and teachers are not required to hold mass final semester exams. If the grade data from summative assessments per topic scope over one semester is sufficient to prove student achievement, teachers no longer need to hold a final semester exam.

::note
**About the Term Summative Exam:** In the field, the term **summative assessment exam** is a popular name for structured evaluations at the end of a topic scope or semester. However, its form does not have to be a written test on paper; art exhibitions or project defenses also have equal legal and pedagogical standing.
::

## The Myth of Standardization and Mastery Learning Principles

Why did the government remove the single KKM number and make final semester exams optional? The answer is rooted in modern pedagogical theory that rejects artificial uniformity.

First, we need to dismantle the myth of the "standardized exam". Grant Wiggins and Jay McTighe in *Understanding by Design* assert that standardized assessments are not identical to computer-based multiple-choice tests. A performance task executed with clear protocols, measurable rubrics, and a fair time limit is also a standardized summative assessment. In fact, this authentic assignment format measures the depth of student reasoning far more accurately than merely guessing answer options.

Second, the KKTP system adopts the philosophy of mastery learning. Sri Rahayu and Nofriansyah in the book *21st Century Learning Models* explain that every student is fundamentally capable of mastering competencies if given the right time allocation and interventions. Summative assessment in the Merdeka Curriculum rejects norm-referenced comparisons that rank students from first to last, comparing student abilities against competency mastery criteria instead (criterion-referenced).

Third, summative evaluation is not a dead end. Yuniastuti, Miftakhuddin, and Khoiron in *Learning Media for the Millennial Generation* explain through the ADDIE (Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, Evaluation) instructional design framework that summative evaluation data at the end of the semester actually becomes the raw input to analyze and design teaching materials for the following semester.

## Guide to Designing Summative Assessments and KKTP

Designing a high-quality final evaluation requires a Backward Design approach. Teachers do not spontaneously create questions the day before the exam, but rather design the achievement evidence beforehand right after formulating the [Learning Outcomes and Learning Objectives](/blog/learning-outcomes-guide).

::steps
### Set the Learning Objectives (TP) to be Measured
Choose one or a combination of several closely related TPs within a topic scope. Ensure the targeted competency contains observable operational verbs that students can tangibly prove.

### Draft the Learning Objective Achievement Criteria (KKTP)
Based on the *2025 Revised Learning and Assessment Guide*, you can choose one of four KKTP approaches:
- **Criteria Description:** A checklist of essential indicators with an "Adequate" or "Inadequate" status.
- **Rubric:** A matrix detailing the quality gradation of student performance (e.g., Beginning, Developing, Competent, Advanced). A.A.I.N. Marhaeni and Luh Putu Artini in *Authentic Assessment in English Learning* emphasize that analytical rubrics are highly effective in maintaining teacher objectivity when assessing students' productive skills.
- **Grade Interval:** A numerical range converted from qualitative criteria to determine follow-up actions (e.g., 0-55 not achieved, 56-70 partially achieved, 71-100 mastered).
- **Percentage:** The proportion of fulfilled indicators out of the total achievement target.

### Choose Authentic Assessment Techniques and Instruments
Adapt the instrument to the demands of the TP. If the TP requires students to analyze social phenomena, use case studies or observation reports. If students learn through a [Project Based Learning (PjBL)](/blog/project-based-learning-guide-and-syntax) scheme, the final project product and its progress journal directly function as summative instruments.

### Conduct the Assessment and Provide Remedial Follow-up
After the assessment is complete, match the student's work with the KKTP. Students who have not reached the minimum threshold have the right to receive specific remedial mentoring on unmastered indicators, rather than repeating the entire test from scratch.
::

## Example of Summative Assessment Application in the Classroom

Many fellow teachers look for practical references regarding what **summative assessment and its examples** are when applied directly to daily subjects. Here is a real case study in Phase C Indonesian Language subject (for 5th or 6th grade).

**Learning Objective (TP):** Students can write structured observation and interview report texts using standard vocabulary and effective sentences.

Instead of giving 30 multiple-choice questions about the definition of an interview, the teacher assigns a portfolio task as a form of final evaluation. Students conduct direct observations at the school cafeteria or local small businesses, interview sources, then compile a written report and present it in front of the class.

Here is an example of the KKTP rubric instrument the teacher uses to grade the summative task:

| Assessment Indicator | Beginning (Score 1) | Developing (Score 2) | Competent (Score 3) | Advanced (Score 4) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Report Text Structure** | Only contains random notes without an opening and conclusion. | Contains report opening and body, but no conclusion paragraph. | Contains a complete structure (opening, observation results body, and conclusion) in order. | Highly complete structure, transitions between paragraphs flow logically and engage the reader. |
| **Data & Fact Accuracy** | The report content does not match the evidence from field interview notes. | A small portion of interview data is included in the report, still mixed with personal opinions. | Observation facts and interview quotes are presented accurately according to field notes. | Accurate, in-depth data, able to distinguish observation facts from source statements. |
| **Linguistic Rules** | Many spelling and punctuation errors that disrupt sentence meaning. | Fairly good spelling, but more than 5 ineffective sentences are still found. | Uses standard vocabulary, correct punctuation, and effective sentences with minor corrections. | Use of standard language, punctuation, and effective sentence structure is highly consistent without flaws. |

**How to Convert to a Numerical Grade:**
The maximum total score from the three indicators above is 12. If a student scores 3 on Text Structure, 3 on Data Accuracy, and 4 on Linguistic Rules, the calculation is:

**(3 + 3 + 4) ÷ 12 × 100 = 83.3 (rounded to 83)**

The teacher sets the mastery threshold if the student is at least in the **Competent category (or a minimum score of 71)**. With a score of 83, the student is declared to have mastered this Learning Objective.

## Processing Summative Assessment Grades into Report Card Grades

Entering the grading period, the most critical question for homeroom teachers is how to formulate the final number. From a regulatory standpoint, **the summative assessment grade is** a quantitative value (whole number) and qualitative value (achievement description) that becomes the sole basis for calculating the final grade in the Merdeka Curriculum report card.

Check out the grade processing simulation of a student named Budi in the IPAS (Natural and Social Sciences) subject over one semester below. During that semester, there are 4 topic scopes (4 TPs), and the school agreed not to hold an End of Semester Summative (SAS) exam because the assessment evidence for each topic is already complete.

| Topic Scope / Learning Objective | Summative Instrument Format | Quantitative Grade | KKTP Status (Mastery Threshold: 70) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **TP 1:** Analyze the water cycle and its impact on earth events | Infographic poster and oral presentation | 88 | Mastered (Very Good) |
| **TP 2:** Demonstrate the change of state of matter due to heat | Simple experimental practice and report sheet | 82 | Mastered (Good) |
| **TP 3:** Identify biotic and abiotic components in a local ecosystem | Open-ended essay written test based on case studies | 76 | Mastered (Good) |
| **TP 4:** Design electrical energy saving solutions in the home environment | Observation log project and props | 65 (Before remedial) → 72 (After intervention) | Needs guidance in the tool design stage |

**Final Report Card Grade Calculation:**
Report Grade = (88 + 82 + 76 + 72) ÷ 4 = **79.5 (rounded to 80)**.

**Drafting the Qualitative Report Description:**
The narrative in the report card no longer repeats long sentences from all TPs, but only takes one highest achievement and one lowest achievement that requires strengthening. For Budi, his report card description reads:
> *"Shows very good mastery in analyzing the water cycle and its impact on earth events. Needs further guidance in designing practical solutions for saving electrical energy in the home environment."*

::tip
**Practical Tips for Homeroom Teachers:** Keep brief notes on student progress as they undergo the remedial process. These notes will greatly help you compose the lowest achievement description sentence in the report card so it sounds constructive, specific, and easy for parents to understand.
::

## 4 Common Teacher Mistakes When Conducting Summative Evaluation

Even though official guides are published regularly, field practices often still fall into old curriculum habits. Avoid the following four traps so your assessments stay aligned with Merdeka Curriculum principles:

### 1. Equating Summative Solely with Multiple-Choice Tests
Many educators still feel it is not a valid grade if they have not distributed multiple-choice question sheets. Forcing students to answer multiple-choice questions to measure their speaking, writing, or sports practice skills actually ruins the assessment's validity. Use performance, product, or portfolio techniques appropriate to the natural characteristics of the competency.

### 2. Mixing Formative Grades into the Report Card Average
The habit of adding up daily homework grades, active participation, and chapter test scores then dividing them equally is wrong. Formative assessment is a safe space for students to make mistakes and learn (trial and error). If formative practice is included in the report card grade formula, students will be afraid to try and will only focus on chasing numbers from the first day of class.

### 3. Forcing Final Semester Exam Week (SAS) When Data is Complete
Organizing a simultaneous final semester exam week often takes up to two full weeks of effective learning time. If you already have strong summative grades at the end of each chapter or topic scope, you have the right to cancel the SAS and use that time for enrichment or project deepening.

### 4. Applying Remedials with the Same Questions Repeatedly
Remedial is not repeating the same test until students memorize the answer key. Before reassessing failed indicators, teachers must provide learning interventions first, for example, through small group tutoring or simplifying learning media, and only then retest the specific unmastered indicator parts.

::faq
  :::faq-item
  ---
  question: Is summative assessment only conducted at the end of the semester?
  ---
  No. Summative assessment is primarily conducted every time one topic scope (one or several Learning Objectives) finishes being taught. End of Semester Summative (SAS) is only an optional supplement if teachers feel they still need additional confirmation of student achievement data.
  :::

  :::faq-item
  ---
  question: Is there a remedial for summative assessment in the Merdeka Curriculum?
  ---
  Yes. Students who have not reached the minimum criteria in the KKTP have the right to receive remedial mentoring before the final grade is determined. Remedial focus is only directed at the unmastered Learning Objective indicators, not repeating the entire material from the beginning.
  :::

  :::faq-item
  ---
  question: Can summative assessment instruments be made differently for each student?
  ---
  Absolutely. Through differentiated instruction principles (specifically product differentiation), students can prove mastery of the same Learning Objective through different media according to their interests and learning profiles, such as written essays, infographic posters, podcast recordings, or video demonstrations, provided they are still measured using an equivalent KKTP rubric.
  :::

  :::faq-item
  ---
  question: How do you determine grade promotion from summative assessment results?
  ---
  The teacher council determines grade promotion through a meeting by considering student achievement in all subjects, behavioral development, and attendance. In the Merdeka Curriculum, students should ideally not be held back a grade; if there are Learning Objectives not mastered by the end of the academic year, those records are forwarded to the next grade teacher to provide further mentoring.
  :::
::

::conclusion
**Action Steps for the Next Evaluation:**
A good summative evaluation is not designed the night before the exam, but prepared from the beginning of learning planning. As a first step this week, reopen your Learning Objective documents for the nearest topic scope, then draft one simple KKTP rubric with four criteria gradations. Replace at least one written test agenda in your class with a performance or authentic product assignment, then observe how students show their understanding more fully and meaningfully.
::
