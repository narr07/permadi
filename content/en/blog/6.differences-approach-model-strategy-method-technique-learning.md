The difference between learning models, strategies, methods, and techniques lies in their operational scope within the classroom. An **approach** acts as the philosophical starting point, a **model** wraps the entire framework of syntax-based activities from start to finish, a **strategy** plans the actions to reach goals, a **method** executes the procedure for delivering materials, and a **technique** manages micro-tactical steps based on real classroom conditions.

In many lesson plans, the method column is often filled with *Problem-Based Learning* (PBL). Elsewhere, *Jigsaw* is frequently written as a model, even though it sits at the technique level. This mapping error is not just a matter of terms on paper. When the boundaries between these concepts blur, classroom scenarios easily lose direction: teachers feel they have applied innovative learning, even though they only distributed a standard worksheet.

Understanding the learning hierarchy helps educators build precise classroom architecture. All these terms work like Russian nesting dolls (*Matryoshka*), covering each other from the largest (macro-philosophical) to the smallest (micro-operational) scale: **Approach → Model → Strategy → Method → Technique → Tactic**.

::note
**Practical Rule to Differentiate the Hierarchy:** If a concept has a standard sequence of steps from introductory to closing activities (syntax), it is a **Model**. If it is a single type of material delivery activity at a specific phase, it is a **Method**.
::

## Breaking Down the Differences Between Learning Models, Strategies, Methods, and Techniques

To avoid confusion when designing lesson plans, we need to dissect each layer from the most philosophical level to the most tactical one in the classroom.

### 1. Learning Approach: The Philosophical Standpoint

A **learning approach** is the philosophical standpoint of educators regarding how students learn. An approach does not discuss technical steps, but rather fundamental beliefs about the roles of teachers and students.

Traditionally, approaches fall into two poles: teacher-centered (rooted in behaviorism) and student-centered (rooted in constructivism).

In Indonesia, the Ministry of Primary and Secondary Education emphasizes a new direction through the **Deep Learning** approach. Many teachers mistakenly think Deep Learning is a new model replacing PBL or PjBL. In reality, Deep Learning sits at the **Approach** level. It rests on three philosophical pillars: Mindful Learning (full awareness and valuing student diversity), Meaningful Learning (relevance of knowledge to the real world), and Joyful Learning (a happy learning atmosphere without administrative pressure).

The same applies to **Differentiated Instruction**. Differentiation is not a method or model, but a philosophical approach that believes every child has unique readiness, interests, and learning profiles.

### 2. Learning Model: The Blueprint with Standard Syntax

A **learning model** is a complete conceptual framework detailing the systematic procedure for organizing learning experiences from the beginning to the end of a session. A model is the largest container that holds approaches, strategies, methods, and techniques.

Referring to the classic work of Bruce Joyce and Marsha Weil in *Models of Teaching*, a new framework can legitimately be called a **Learning Model** if it meets five main pillars:
1. **Syntax:** The standard and clearly written sequence of activity phases or steps.
2. **Social System:** The relationship patterns, ground rules, and roles between teachers and students.
3. **Principles of Reaction:** How teachers respond, help, and assess student behavior or answers.
4. **Support System:** The required facilities, teaching materials, media, and learning environment.
5. **Instructional and Nurturant Effects:** The direct learning outcomes aligning with Learning Objectives and the character traits that grow indirectly.

Joyce and Weil group various [types of innovative learning models](/blog/types-of-innovative-learning-models) into four major families: the Information Processing Family (like Inquiry and Discovery Learning), the Social Family (like Cooperative Learning and Role Playing), the Personal Family (Non-directive Teaching), and the Behavioral Systems Family (Direct Instruction).

When you choose [Problem Based Learning stages and practical syntax](/blog/problem-based-learning-stages-and-syntax) or a [Project Based Learning guide and PjBL syntax](/blog/project-based-learning-guide-and-syntax), you are setting the architectural blueprint of the class from the first minute until the dismissal bell rings.

### 3. Learning Strategy: The Operational Master Plan

Curriculum expert Wina Senjaya (2008) defines a learning strategy as "a plan of operation achieving something". If a model is the building blueprint, a **learning strategy** is the tactical plan regarding the sequence of activities, material organization, and time allocation to achieve the Learning Objectives.

The two most frequently used strategy poles in class include:
* **Expository Strategy:** The teacher thoroughly processes the material and presents it in a structured way so students master basic concepts quickly and accurately.
* **Heuristic / Inquiry Strategy:** The teacher designs scenarios so students search, investigate, and discover material conclusions themselves through a series of problems.

In differentiated lesson plans, content, process, and product differentiation work at this **Strategy** level. Teachers plan how to group students and arrange variations in their learning paths within the ongoing model.

### 4. Learning Method: The Material Delivery Procedure

If a strategy is a plan on the teacher's desk, then a **learning method** is "a way in achieving something", which is the procedural workflow directly executed in front of students to realize that strategy.

One learning model almost certainly requires a combination of several methods. For example, when running the first phase of the PBL syntax (Problem Orientation), the teacher uses the **Q&A method** and **demonstration method**. Entering the third phase (Guiding the Investigation), the teacher switches to using the **experiment method** or **field observation method**. In the fourth phase, the teacher applies the **presentation method** and **discussion method**.

So, interactive lectures, demonstrations, group discussions, simulations, field trips, and drills all hold the status of methods.

### 5. Learning Techniques and Tactics: Micro Variations and Personal Style

A **learning technique** is a specific, operational, and situational way chosen by a teacher when executing a method to fit the real conditions of the classroom. Two teachers might both use the **discussion method**, but apply different **active learning techniques** because the number of students and classroom sizes are not the same.

Teacher A teaching a large class in a tight space chooses the **Think-Pair-Share technique** (think independently, discuss with a seatmate, then share with the class). Meanwhile, Teacher B teaching a small class in a spacious room chooses the **Fishbowl technique** (two-layered circle) or the **Jigsaw technique** (dividing home teams and expert teams). Other popular techniques include Gallery Walk, Two Stay Two Stray, and the Pause Procedure technique (giving brief pauses during lectures for Q&A).

At the very outer edge lies the **Learning Tactic**. A tactic is a teacher's unique style and personal spontaneity when executing techniques in the field. Using dramatic voice intonation, inserting local humor to break the ice, making eye contact, and managing speaking tempo are tactics. Tactics cannot be copied raw from a Lesson Plan document because they blend with the personality and experience of each educator.

## Learning Concept Hierarchy Comparison Table

To make it easier to map the differences between approaches, strategies, methods, models, and techniques when preparing lesson plans, pay attention to this comparison matrix of the six pedagogical concept levels:

| Hierarchy Level | Nature & Scope | Operational Definition | Teacher's Key Question | Concrete Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Approach** | Philosophical (Very Macro) | The viewpoint or basic belief about the nature of the student learning process. | *What is my perspective on how students build knowledge?* | Constructivism, Student-Centered, Deep Learning, Differentiated. |
| **Model** | Complete Conceptual Framework (Macro) | The instructional design from start to finish that has 5 main pillars, including standard syntax. | *What does the blueprint and standard stages of the class look like from opening to closing?* | Problem-Based Learning (PBL), PjBL, Discovery Learning, Direct Instruction. |
| **Strategy** | Tactical Master Plan (Meso) | Structured action plans related to material sequencing, time allocation, and student grouping. | *What path plan is most effective to achieve target competencies?* | Inquiry Strategy, Expository, Process/Product Differentiation Strategy. |
| **Method** | Class Procedural (Micro-Procedural) | Real and systematic ways to present material or help activities in each syntax phase. | *What concrete way do I use in this phase to interact with students?* | Group discussion, Interactive lecture, Demonstration, Experiment, Simulation. |
| **Technique** | Operational & Situational (Micro) | Specific steps in running a method, adjusted to real class conditions. | *What are the specific rules so this discussion method is not passive?* | Think-Pair-Share, Jigsaw, Gallery Walk, Mind Mapping, Fishbowl. |
| **Tactic** | Personal & Artistic (Individual) | The touch of art, improvisation, and unique style of individual teachers when managing class psychology. | *What personal touch makes students enthusiastic about this technique?* | Local humor analogy, whispering vocal intonation during key points, spontaneous ice-breaking. |

## Combining Standard Syntax with Differentiation Flexibility

One common complaint among educators when implementing the Merdeka Curriculum is feeling restricted by model syntax. Many ask: "If I have to follow the 5 steps of PBL syntax sequentially, where is the flexibility of differentiated instruction?"

This is where understanding the **differences between learning models, strategies, methods, and techniques** becomes useful. The syntax of a learning model is a logical framework of thinking, not a bureaucratic prison. Syntax ensures the students' cognitive flow moves systematically from problem introduction to drawing conclusions.

We execute differentiation flexibility at the **strategy, method, and technique** levels within each of these syntax stages.

For example, in the 3rd Syntax of PBL (Guiding individual and group investigations), you do not change the stage name. However, within that stage, you apply a **process differentiation strategy**:
* Groups of students with basic learning readiness are helped through the **direct demonstration method** and **step-by-step guidance technique (scaffolding)**.
* Groups of students who are already proficient investigate problems through the **independent literature study method** with **case analysis techniques**.

The same goes for the Deep Learning approach. You do not need to look for a "Deep Learning Syntax" because the concept is not a learning model. You simply choose any relevant model (it could be PBL, [discovery learning and inquiry stages for class](/blog/discovery-learning-and-inquiry-stages-for-class), or Cooperative Learning), then make sure the Merdeka Curriculum learning methods and techniques chosen within it make students fully aware (mindful), feel real benefits (meaningful), and enjoy the process (joyful).

## Example of Integrated Application in a Lesson Plan

Many references only mention concept examples separately, leaving teachers confused when they have to assemble them into a single, complete Lesson Plan document. We can dissect a real scenario in a Science subject on the topic of **Water Ecosystem Pollution**.

**Learning Objective:** Students can analyze the impact of household waste on aquatic ecosystems in the environment around the school and design solution ideas.

Here is how to unite the entire hierarchy of these concepts into a workflow:

::steps
### Setting the Approach and Model as the Main Umbrella
* **Approach:** Constructivism with a Deep Learning orientation (meaningful and contextual) and Differentiated Instruction.
* **Learning Model:** Problem-Based Learning (PBL). This model is chosen because the learning objective demands solving a real problem in the environment around the school, not just memorizing the definition of water pollution.

### Designing the Learning Strategy
* **Strategy:** Inquiry-Discovery strategy based on collaborative work in heterogeneous groups to accommodate student learning profile differentiation. The teacher sets time allocation for problem orientation, water sample investigation, up to the exhibition of work.

### Executing Methods, Techniques, and Tactics Within the PBL Syntax
* **PBL Phase 1 (Problem Orientation):**
  * *Method:* Demonstration and Discussion.
  * *Technique:* Think-Pair-Share (ensuring students who tend to be quiet still actively talk with their seatmates before entering the class discussion).
  * *Tactic:* The teacher opens the orientation by bringing a bottle of cloudy ditch water to the front desk, then greets students with an analogy of humor relevant to their lives in the canteen and the environment around the school.
* **PBL Phase 2 (Organizing Students):**
  * *Method:* Group Discussion.
  * *Technique:* Assigning specific roles within heterogeneous groups.
* **PBL Phase 3 (Guiding the Investigation):**
  * *Method:* Field Observation and Simple Experiment (testing the clarity, odor, and pH level of water samples).
  * *Technique:* Scaffolding (groups needing guidance are given detailed work step guides, while advanced groups design their observation tables independently).
* **PBL Phase 4 (Developing and Presenting Work):**
  * *Method:* Presentation and Performance.
  * *Technique:* Gallery Walk. Each group exhibits their water pH test data and solution designs, while representatives from other groups walk around to give feedback.
* **PBL Phase 5 (Analyzing and Evaluating the Problem-Solving Process):**
  * *Method:* Evaluative Discussion and Q&A.
  * *Technique:* Guided reflection to conclude the effectiveness of the solutions designed together.
::

From the scenario above, it is clear that the PBL model remains intact with its five syntaxes, yet it becomes alive and dynamic thanks to the combination of various methods, techniques, and teacher tactics in each phase.

## 4 Common Mistakes When Preparing Lesson Plans and How to Fix Them

When reviewing teaching materials, there are four conceptual traps that most frequently recur and make lesson plans less sharp:

### 1. Writing PBL or PjBL in the Learning Method Column
Many lesson plans get stuck during curriculum review because the general information section says: *"Learning Method: Problem-Based Learning"*.
* **Correction:** Separate the rows for **Learning Model** and **Learning Method**. Write *Problem-Based Learning* in the Model row because it has syntax phases 1 to 5, then write *Discussion, Experiment, and Q&A* in the Method row.

### 2. Assuming Worksheet Distribution is Automatically Discovery Learning
Many educators feel they have applied the *Discovery Learning* model simply by distributing Student Worksheets containing fill-in-the-blank questions that students must answer using a textbook.
* **Correction:** A model includes a social system and principles of teacher reaction. *Discovery Learning* demands dialogic support as well as stages of giving stimulation, problem identification, up to verification. Without these syntax stages, the activity of filling out worksheets is just a regular independent assignment method.

### 3. Forcing Project Models (PjBL) for All Types of Materials
Because PjBL is often recommended in curriculum training, an assumption arises that all chapters must be taught through projects. However, forcing a complex multi-week project for pure fact materials or basic procedural skills (like basic early-stage algebra formulas) actually wastes time and blurs core concepts.
* **Correction:** Match the model with the Learning Outcomes demands. For basic procedural knowledge, the **Direct Instruction Model** combined with leveled practice techniques (scaffolding) is far more effective and on target.

### 4. Confusing Discussion Techniques with Discussion Methods
Writing *"Method: Jigsaw and Think-Pair-Share"* in a lesson plan narrows the class planning structure.
* **Correction:** The method is **Group Discussion**, while how to divide expert teams and home teams is the **Jigsaw Technique**.

::warning
**Avoid the Shallow Learning Trap:** Using too many game techniques in one meeting without the bond of a clear model and strategy often makes the class seem noisy and fun (joyful), but fails to achieve deep concept understanding (meaningful). Make sure every chosen technique truly serves the learning objective.
::

## Diagnostic Guide for Choosing a Learning Hierarchy

How do you quickly determine a combination of models, methods, and techniques when faced with a new Learning Objective? Use this diagnostic table based on target competencies:

| Main Target Competency (LO) | Recommended Model | Recommended Strategy | Primary Method Combination | Specific Technique Options |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Understanding basic concepts & standard procedures** | Direct Instruction or Guided Discovery | Step-by-step Expository Strategy | Meaningful Lecture, Demonstration, Drill. | Word-Clue, Q&A, Pause Procedure, Scaffolding. |
| **Independent concept discovery from phenomena** | Discovery Learning or Inquiry Learning | Heuristic / Inquiry Strategy | Observation, Experiment, Discussion. | Concept Attainment, Think-Pair-Share. |
| **Critical reasoning & solving real cases** | Problem-Based Learning (PBL) | Collaborative Investigation Strategy | Discussion, Field Investigation, Literature Study. | Fishbowl, Six Thinking Hats, Gallery Walk. |
| **Real product creation & project management** | Project-Based Learning (PjBL) | Project-Based Collaborative Strategy | Work Design, Experiment, Presentation. | Gallery Walk, Exhibition of Work. |
| **Social empathy, negotiation, & teamwork** | Role Playing or Cooperative Learning | Heterogeneous Cooperative Strategy | Simulation, Group Discussion. | Role Reversal, Jigsaw. |

::faq
  :::faq-item
  ---
  question: What is the most fundamental difference between a learning model and a learning method?
  ---
  A learning model is a complete design framework from the beginning to the end of a session that must have standard step stages or syntax (like PBL or PjBL). Meanwhile, a method is a procedural way to deliver material at one specific phase within that model (such as a lecture, discussion, or demonstration). A single learning model can contain several methods at once.
  :::

  :::faq-item
  ---
  question: Does the Merdeka Curriculum mandate one specific learning model?
  ---
  No. The Learning and Assessment Guide for the Merdeka Curriculum gives teachers the freedom to choose models that align with Learning Outcomes and student needs. The Merdeka Curriculum encourages student-centered models like PBL, PjBL, Discovery, and Inquiry to strengthen critical reasoning and collaboration, but models like Direct Instruction remain valid for basic concept materials.
  :::

  :::faq-item
  ---
  question: Why is the lecture method often considered poor in modern learning?
  ---
  The lecture method is essentially not a bad method. What is problematic is a prolonged one-way lecture (passive monologue) without student involvement, which results in low learning retention. Interactive lecturing combined with pause procedures or Q&A remains effective for explaining basic concepts.
  :::

  :::faq-item
  ---
  question: Where do Deep Learning and Differentiated Instruction fit in this hierarchy?
  ---
  Both sit at the **Learning Approach** level, not models or methods. Deep Learning (Mindful, Meaningful, Joyful) and Differentiated Instruction are philosophies of teacher perspective which are then operationalized through differentiation strategies (content, process, product) and executed through various choices of models, methods, and techniques in the classroom.
  :::
::

::conclusion
Mastering the **differences between learning models, strategies, methods, and techniques** frees educators from administrative confusion when drafting Lesson Plans. As an action step for your next class meeting, reopen your lesson plan draft and do these three quick checks:
1. Make sure the **Model** column is only filled by frameworks that have complete syntax from the beginning to the end of the session.
2. Check whether the syntax stages in the core activities are already supported by a variety of active **Methods**, not just one-way assignments.
3. Choose at least one specific **Technique** (like Think-Pair-Share or Gallery Walk) so every student, including the quietest in the class, has measurable and meaningful room for participation.
::
