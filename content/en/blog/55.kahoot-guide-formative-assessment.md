![Illustration of a teacher laptop and projector displaying an interactive Kahoot quiz in a classroom](/article/edu-kahoot-asesmen-kelas.webp)

**Kahoot** is a game-based learning platform that helps teachers create interactive quizzes, surveys, and visual discussions for both real-time and independent study. Students join simply by entering a unique PIN code in their browser without needing an account. This allows teachers to run gamified formative assessments that diagnose class understanding instantly.

Many educators still treat these digital quizzes merely as a fun break in the last ten minutes of class. However, when aligned with instructional goals, the projector screen and those four iconic colored buttons transform into a sharp diagnostic instrument. This article explains how to design, help run, and analyze interactive Kahoot quiz sessions in the classroom, complete with ways to use its latest features for deep and differentiated learning.

## Why Is Kahoot Effective as an Interactive Learning Medium?

Conventional classrooms often get stuck in one-way communication where the teacher explains the material and only two or three of the bravest students raise their hands during Q&A sessions. Using interactive learning media breaks this ice because it requires every student to make cognitive decisions at the same time.

Prof. Dr. M. Sobry Sutikno, in his book *Learning Media: From Theory to Practice*, emphasizes that effective teaching media can turn passive dynamics into active, student-centered learning. The combination of visuals on the main screen, rhythmic audio that builds focus, and direct feedback on device screens provides enjoyable cognitive stimulation that keeps learning attention high.

From a game pedagogy perspective, Elsi Sirampun and Hermin explain in *Learning Models: Theory, Practice, and Innovation* that a game-based learning approach provides a safe space for students to try and make mistakes (trial and error). When teachers package evaluations into game mechanics, test anxiety drops drastically. Students no longer view teacher questions as interrogations that determine their report card grades, but rather as puzzle challenges that sharpen their problem-solving skills.

The main strength of this platform lies in its function as a practical [formative assessment in the classroom](/blog/formative-assessment-in-classroom). A.A.I.N. Marhaeni and Luh Putu Artini in *Authentic Assessment in English Learning* state that the essence of formative assessment (assessment for learning and assessment as learning) is not to judge the final result, but to monitor the learning process, detect misconceptions instantly, and trigger student self-reflection. Every time the bar chart of answer distribution appears on the projector after a question ends, the teacher immediately gathers empirical data: whether the class is ready to move on to the next material or needs a re-explanation of a specific concept.

## New Kahoot Features That Change Classroom Dynamics

For years, the biggest criticism of gamified quizzes in the classroom has been speed bias. Students with fast motor reflexes and the most stable internet connections often dominate the leaderboard, while students who need time to process reading materials fall behind and lose motivation.

![Visual diagram of a four-quadrant diagnostic model for accuracy and confidence in digital quizzes](/article/edu-kahoot-asesmen-kelas-ilustrasi.webp)

Through a series of platform updates until late 2025 and throughout 2026, the way these quizzes work has shifted from a simple thumb-speed contest to cognitive fairness. Here is a comparison of the game modes now available to accommodate [differentiated instruction](/blog/differentiated-instruction-guide) in your classroom:

| Game Mode | Scoring Mechanism & Display | Student Device Requirements | Best Pedagogical Use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Classic Mode** | Points are calculated from correct answers and response speed. | 1 device per student (1:1). | Ice breaking, quick fact reviews, or basic concept fluency practice. |
| **Accuracy Experience** | Points purely from answer accuracy with no time penalty; option to turn off the timer. | 1 device per student or in groups. | High-order thinking questions (HOTS), long text literacy, and inclusive classrooms. |
| **Confidence Experience** | Students choose their confidence level before locking in a multiple-choice answer. | 1 device per student. | Training metacognition and distinguishing lucky guesses from misconceptions. |
| **Unplugged (Classic / Team Tiles)** | Questions appear on the teacher's projector, physical group discussion, teacher inputs team answers. | No student devices (0 devices). | Schools with limited signal, phone bans, or group discussions. |
| **Kahootopia Assignments** | The whole class collects points together to build a collective digital island. | Independent (synchronous or asynchronous homework). | Building a cooperative culture without the pressure of an individual leaderboard. |

### Deconstructing Speed with Accuracy Experience

The Accuracy Experience feature fundamentally changes the rules of the game. Teachers can now remove the speed bonus component so every student who answers correctly earns identical points, regardless of how many seconds they spend before time runs out. You can even turn off the timer completely (unlimited answer time). This feature really helps neurodiverse students process information at their own pace, allowing them to think calmly without the pressure of a ticking clock on the screen.

### Training Metacognition with Confidence Experience

Introduced in mid-2026, the Confidence Experience mode asks students to gauge how sure they are about their selected answers. From a formative assessment perspective, this feature maps students into four highly valuable diagnostic quadrants for teachers:

1. **Correct answer and very confident:** Concept mastery is solid.
2. **Correct answer but hesitant:** Indication of a lucky guess. The concept needs strengthening so it does not easily falter.
3. **Wrong answer and hesitant:** Students realize they do not understand yet. They are ready to receive scaffolding help.
4. **Wrong answer but very confident:** This is the most dangerous misconception trap. Students feel their knowledge is correct, but their logical foundation is flawed.

### Solving the Digital Divide with Unplugged Mode

Not all schools have even Wi-Fi coverage or allow elementary and middle school students to bring smartphones every day. The release of the Classic: Unplugged and Team Tiles: Unplugged features in September 2026 answers this challenge. Teachers simply display the quiz on the front projector screen, divide students into discussion teams, and ask each team to hold up a physical colored card (red triangle, blue diamond, yellow circle, or green square). The teacher then inputs each team's answer directly from the teaching laptop.

## Steps to Design Quiz Sessions Aligned with Deep Learning

The Deep Learning framework in the Merdeka Curriculum demands learning experiences that are mindful, meaningful, and joyful. Referring to the *Deep Learning* guidebook, educators hold three key roles: as an Activator who stimulates learning targets, a Collaborator who helps facilitate inquiry, and a Learning Culture Developer who creates a safe space for students to take intellectual risks.

To ensure your interactive Kahoot quiz sessions reflect these principles, follow this workflow in your classroom:

::steps
### Map Learning Goals and Create Contextual Stimulus
Do not just type questions into the app dashboard right away. Reopen your [learning outcomes](/blog/learning-outcomes-guide). Avoid designing a quiz filled entirely with date memorization or dictionary definitions (Low Order Thinking Skills). Use microscopic images, data chart snippets, blank maps, or short everyday cases as a stimulus before the question sentence. If you use the AI Question Generator feature by uploading a teaching material PDF, always edit the AI-generated draft so the distractor options truly represent the logical errors your students often make.

### Choose the Mode According to the Instructional Phase
Adjust the technical settings to the quiz's position in your teaching flow. In the first 10 minutes of the lesson (cognitive diagnostic assessment), use the Poll / Survey mode or Accuracy Experience without scores to provoke curiosity without the fear of being wrong. In the middle of the session (check for understanding), insert 3 to 5 questions featuring the Confidence Experience. At the end of the session to reinforce the material, run Team Mode where one phone is shared by 3 to 4 students. This creates healthy debate and peer instruction before the team agrees to press one colored button.

### Conduct Deep Debriefing at Every Question Pause
The biggest mistake quiz hosts make is rushing to press the Next button as soon as the answer chart appears. Pause for a moment whenever you see many students fall for a distractor option. Use the answer distribution chart on the projector as material for class discussion. Ask students why they chose that option with a warm and curious tone without embarrassing them, then discuss the key concepts together. This open dialogue turns an ordinary quiz into a meaningful learning moment.

### Download Reports for Differentiation in the Next Meeting
After the game finishes, open the Reports menu on your account dashboard and download the analysis sheet in Excel or PDF format. Yuniastuti et al. in *Learning Media for the Millennial Generation* remind us that teachers must follow up on the digital footprint of interactive media to make instructional decisions. Pay attention to the Difficult Questions list, which contains questions with a success rate below 60 percent. Use this data to map remedial materials or design process differentiation for the next day's meeting.
::

## Five Common Traps When Running Quizzes in Class

Many teachers complain about classroom atmospheres that become chaotic, noisy without substance, or make smart students dominate even more while slower students give up mid-game. Here are five of the most frequent practical mistakes and how to fix them:

1. **Speed over Substance Syndrome:** Giving too little time for questions that require reading analysis makes students guess randomly to chase speed combos. Fix this by activating Accuracy Experience or extending the countdown duration to 60 to 90 seconds per question.
2. **Motivation-Killing Leaderboards:** Students who fall to the bottom early in the game often stop thinking and just press buttons randomly until the session ends. Use Kahootopia Assignments or Team Mode so achievements are collective, or turn off the periodic leaderboard display between questions.
3. **Disruptive Nicknames:** Letting students type their own names sometimes triggers the use of mocking words that disrupt class focus. Activate the Friendly Nickname Generator feature in the lobby settings so the system automatically draws safe and fun pseudonyms.
4. **Paralysis When School Internet Fails:** Relying on each student's cellular connection inside thick-walled classrooms often causes participants to get thrown out of the game session. Keep a set of cardboard color cards on the teacher's desk; as soon as the network slows down, you can immediately switch to Unplugged mode without canceling the prepared quiz.
5. **Using Quizzes as a Closer Without Follow-Up:** Running a quiz just minutes before the dismissal bell rings leaves the teacher with no time to discuss questions students failed to answer. Leave at least 10 minutes after the quiz ends specifically to reteach concepts the class has not fully mastered.

::tip
**Integration into Teaching Modules:** You can embed the Assign / Challenge session link directly into the attachments of your [Merdeka teaching modules](/blog/merdeka-teaching-module-guide). This way, students who are absent from school can still access the same formative assessment independently from home according to the deadline you set.

::faq
  :::faq-item
  ---
  question: Can Kahoot be used for free for a full class?
  ---
  Yes. A free account with an elementary and middle school teacher profile (K-12 Teacher) gets a capacity of up to 40 participants per live session. This quota is sufficient for a standard class size. Make sure you select the Teacher category and School institution during initial registration.
  :::

  :::faq-item
  ---
  question: How do students join a quiz without downloading the app?
  ---
  Students do not need to install an app or register for any account. They simply open a web browser on their phone or laptop, go to kahoot.it, and enter the 6 to 7-digit PIN code displayed on the teacher's projector screen along with their nickname.
  :::

  :::faq-item
  ---
  question: What is the fundamental difference between Kahoot and Quizizz in the classroom?
  ---
  By original design, Kahoot prioritizes shared-screen interaction where questions appear on the front projector to spark simultaneous social interaction in face-to-face classes, whereas Quizizz displays questions and answers directly on each student's device screen independently (self-paced). Currently, both platforms have adopted similar features from one another.
  :::

  :::faq-item
  ---
  question: How do I use Kahoot for independent homework?
  ---
  Open the quiz you have created, select the Assign / Challenge option, and set the deadline date and time. Share the assignment link or PIN code with the class group. In this asynchronous mode, questions and answer choices will appear fully on each student's device screen so they can complete it independently from home.
  :::

::conclusion
Using Kahoot effectively requires a change in perspective: from just a game to fill free time into a planned diagnostic instrument. Start taking practical steps this week by picking one of your old quizzes, changing its settings to Accuracy Experience or Confidence Experience mode, and replacing at least three memorization questions with short case study-based questions. Take ten minutes after the quiz finishes to discuss the distractor options with students, then experience for yourself how the classroom atmosphere becomes more lively, equal, and reflective.
